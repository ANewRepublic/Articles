# Citizenship

Citizens are adults who have taken the oath and hold a current credential. A stake in the nation, not a vacation, is what carries a national vote. The credential is how the nation recognizes one person, one weight, without keeping a readable catalog of the people.

## The oath

The oath is:

> I take this constitution as the law of the nation. I will not use force to overturn it outside its own amendment rules. I may argue, vote, and seek its amendment.

The oath binds the citizen to the constitution, not to the statutes of the day. Those change. Arguing for their repeal, and arguing harshly about the nation, is an exercise of speech and not a breach.

This is the only civic oath. A sub-jurisdiction does not administer an oath and does not ask or pressure anyone to swear loyalty to the charter, to promise to remain, or to promise not to seek its change. A private religious vow is not a civic oath and is not a condition of living in a place, owning land, voting, leaving, or speaking. Where a charter may require an officer to be a member of a religion, that membership is not an oath to the sub-jurisdiction.

## Applying

An applicant appears in person and:

- takes the oath
- provides a biological sample used only as the technical elements below require

A nominal fee may be charged. The fee is waived for an applicant who cannot pay it. A public terminal at an enrollment office can receive the credential, so buying a personal device is not a condition of citizenship. The terminal keeps no identity after the ceremony.

The founding commit may record the first citizens as having taken this oath.

## Renewing

Citizenship is renewed every five years, in person. Renewal requires the oath again, and either a new biological sample under the same rules or proof of possession of the current credential.

Renewal never incurs a fee.

Renewal confirms that a living person still claims membership. It is also when the citizen's primary dwelling is recorded for the vote.

## Who may vote

Voting weight in the nation belongs to a citizen whose primary dwelling is in the nation.

It also belongs to a citizen whose primary dwelling is elsewhere, for as long as that citizen keeps a national tie and renews citizenship in person. Renewal is every five years. A citizen who keeps a home, land, or a business here, and who appears for that renewal, does not lose the national vote for living abroad in the meantime. There is no shorter cutoff while the tie and the renewals continue.

A citizen abroad with no national tie keeps national voting weight for two years from the start of the absence, and then it pauses. A visit that does not restore a dwelling, owned land, or an operating business does not restart the two years and does not restore a paused vote. The vote returns when the citizen again has a primary dwelling in the nation or a national tie.

A sub-jurisdiction gives one vote to each of its resident citizens. Those ballots set the rules of daily life for the people who live there.

A charter may also give one vote to other citizens, on a general condition the charter states in plain words. Ownership of land in the territory is one such condition. The condition treats every citizen alike. A larger holding, or a second parcel, does not add a vote. Owning a parcel there, or operating a business there, keeps the national vote under the rule above. It casts the town's ballots only when the charter extends this one vote.

Citizenship itself continues while the credential is renewed, even when voting weight is paused. The rights of a citizen do not depend on the vote.

An unexcused refusal of council service or of jury service pauses voting weight for the period those articles state. Incarceration does not, by itself, remove citizenship or national voting weight. The jail provides the private voting terminal.

No sub-jurisdiction adds or removes the national rules of voting weight. It cannot give a vote to a non-citizen, it cannot take the one vote from a resident citizen, and it cannot give any person more than one vote in that sub-jurisdiction.

## Proof of citizenship

Each citizen holds a cryptographic credential. It can prove, at the citizen's choice, any subset of:

- identity, to a verifier the citizen chooses
- citizenship
- adulthood
- eligibility to vote or to delegate
- eligibility to receive an arm, as the rights article defines that fact

A third party may accept a zero-knowledge proof from the credential. A site may use one to gate adult material, or to allow one account for one adult. Each proof is unlinkable to other verifiers unless the citizen chooses to be linkable. The nation does not receive a log of those proofs.

The credential is bound to a device. It cannot be exported. Handing the device or the secret to another person or to an agent voids the credential. Citizenship continues. Voting weight pauses until the citizen re-enrolls in person. An agent never holds a credential and never becomes a citizen or a delegate.

## Loss of the credential

Loss is reported to an enrollment office. The lost credential is revoked. Citizenship continues. Voting weight pauses until re-issue, so a lost device cannot be used to vote.

Re-issue uses the biological proof below. The old commitment is marked ended. The person remains one person in the set of citizens.

## Renouncing

A citizen may renounce in person, with the biological proof and the credential if it is still possessed. Citizenship and voting weight end immediately. The person may leave with personal property. Land still owned does not stay in a non-citizen's title. Within one year it is transferred to a citizen or a legal entity, or it is sold under the taxation statute and the surplus is returned. Until that transfer, the land tax continues and the former citizen has no vote.

## Ending citizenship against the holder's will

Citizenship ends without consent only when:

- the citizen does not renew, in which case it lapses rather than being punished
- a final national judgment finds enrollment fraud, or a false declaration of residence made to capture a ballot
- a final national judgment finds treason, or that the citizen levied armed force against the nation

Public renunciation, contempt for the nation, and advocacy of amendment or even of a different constitution are not grounds. They are speech. The oath's promise is about force, and only a judgment about force or fraud reaches the credential.

## Minors

Minors are guest citizens when at least one guardian is a citizen. They carry no vote and no second credential that a guardian could vote with. A minor who is not a guest citizen still has the rights of a person, and the parental rules in the rights article still apply to that minor's guardians.

## Non-citizens

A person who is not a citizen has the rights of a person inside the borders, except the citizen's vote and credential. Presence inside the borders is governed by the defense article. Long residence is a reason to offer the oath, not a substitute for it.

## Technical elements

The biological sample is an input to a zero-knowledge enrollment and nothing else. It is destroyed after the ceremony, in the presence of the applicant. The destruction is part of the ceremony, not a later promise. The nation keeps no raw biometric and no readable catalog of citizens.

The enrollment proves two things without revealing the set:

- this is the same person on a later re-issue or renewal that uses a sample
- this person is not already enrolled

Hardware designs and software for the ceremony are published. A citizen may run the verification software. An officer who keeps a sample, a template, or a parallel list of identities commits a national crime under the criminal statute.

Between renewals the governance record stores commitments. It does not store names as a public roll of the people.
