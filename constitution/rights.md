# Rights

"Your freedom ends where my nose begins."

Rights are liberties of persons. They are intrinsic to human life. A person may consent to a particular act that would otherwise violate a right held by that person. No statute, charter, officer, or court extinguishes a right. Only a constitutional change can narrow a right, and the constitutional ballot is the consent of the governed to that narrowing.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Rights never create or guarantee goods, services, or wealth. A service a sub-jurisdiction chooses to offer is a program. It is not a right, and ending it for the future is not a taking.

## How a right is read

A court reads a right in this article by the words written here. It does not add an exception the words do not state. It does not invent a right to goods, services, or wealth. Silence in this constitution is not a power to shrink a listed right. A phrase that does not appear here is not a rule. "Separation of church and state" does not appear here.

Rights bind the nation and every sub-jurisdiction. They bind officers in and out of uniform. They apply to every person inside the borders. Voting and holding a credential are rights of citizens, and the rest of this article is not limited to citizens.

## Life, body, and defense

A person has the right to life and to bodily integrity.

No person is compelled to undergo a medical or surgical procedure. A court may authorize care for a person who cannot consent, and only to prevent an imminent threat of death or serious permanent injury. An adult who can consent may refuse care, including when refusal risks death.

A person may use proportionate force to stop an imminent unlawful attack on that person, on another person, or on property the person has a right to defend. There is no duty to retreat from a place the person has a right to be. The right is a defense in any court of the nation. It does not license pursuit or punishment after the threat has ended.

## Slavery

Neither slavery nor involuntary servitude exists in the nation or in any sub-jurisdiction. Compulsory short service on a jury, or on the council, under the articles that create those duties, is not servitude. Prison labor is limited by the [incarceration](../statutes/incarceration.md) statute.

## Speech, press, petition, and assembly

A person may speak, print, publish, petition, assemble peaceably, and associate, and may display words and images other than an indecent image. Speech includes political argument, religious argument, news, scientific argument, and criticism of government, including criticism that describes sex.

This right does not cover:

- a true threat of unlawful violence against a particular person
- fraud
- incitement of an imminent violent crime
- sexual abuse material of a minor
- exposure of genitals, or sexual conduct, in a public place
- a visual record whose primary content is sexual conduct or exposed genitals, offered or displayed as sexual display

Exposure of a person's body is not speech. Calling it a protest does not make it speech. An indecent image, as the last item above describes it, is not speech. Words remain speech when they describe sex. A picture whose purpose is news of a war or of a public event, and not sexual display, is not an indecent image.

What public exposure is punished, and what public display or distribution of an indecent image is punished, is defined by the charter of the place, in words an ordinary adult can apply. The nation does not keep its own definition of either. Sexual abuse material of a minor remains a national crime. On land with no charter, no exposure rule and no image rule of this kind is in force. A statute that treats political or religious criticism as indecency is not applied.

A sub-jurisdiction may regulate other conduct in public places it governs, including the time, place, and manner of assemblies, if the regulation leaves ample ways to speak and is not a rule about the viewpoint. No law punishes speech, reading, worship, or quiet possession of writings inside a home, other than a true threat, fraud, incitement of an imminent violent crime, or sexual abuse material of a minor. Private possession of an indecent image is not a national crime. A local rule against that possession which can be carried out only by searching a home is not a lawful plan under the criminal article.

Petition to the nation is free. A fee for ranking in a hosted forum is not a condition of petition.

No person is compelled to profess a belief, or to speak.

No level of government imposes a prior restraint on publication. A court may punish a completed crime that used words, after the fact, within the exclusions above.

## Religion

A person may hold a religion or none, may worship or decline to worship, and may change religion.

Free exercise is not confined to a home. A person may speak and act on religion in a public place, may wear religious dress, may carry and display religious symbols, and may gather with others for worship, procession, and teaching. That freedom, together with the freedom of assembly, includes forming a church or another religious body and maintaining a building on land the body has a right to use.

No level of government establishes a church. No level prohibits a religion, punishes a person for holding a faith, or confines worship to a private house. Private worship is lawful in every sub-jurisdiction. A congregation may meet on private land it has a right to use.

Nothing in this constitution clears religion out of public property, out of public speech, or out of the law's own words. A government may use religious language and religious symbols. Religious emblems may remain on public property, and may be placed there by the same process that governs other uses of that property.

The nation requires no religious test for citizenship, a vote, a delegation, a jury, the council, a court, or any national trust.

A charter may be the charter of a religious town. It may require its own officers to be members of a stated religion, and it may set the public ceremonies of that town. It may not apply that test to residence, ownership, the vote, jury service, passage, exit, or the national oath. It may not compel support of a house of worship. It may not forbid private worship, and it may not stop a congregation from meeting on private land the congregation has a right to use. It may refuse public land and public funds to a faith other than its own.

## Arms

An adult has the right to keep and bear arms for defense of self, home, and community, and as a check on tyranny. A minor keeps and bears arms only under the direction of a parent. No statute, charter, or officer raises the age above adulthood.

The right does not depend on a permit, a registry, a mental-health list, or a medical opinion. No level of government keeps a list of who owns arms. No level makes prior permission, a doctor's note, or a prediction of future danger a condition of keeping or bearing common arms. A charter cannot forbid keeping arms, cannot forbid bearing them in public, and cannot forbid citizens from training together in their use.

The right is suspended only while a person is confined after a criminal conviction, and only inside a jail that meets the booth rule below. Release restores the right. An arrest, a charge, and a civil commitment do not suspend it.

Training is voluntary. It is not enrollment in a force and it is not conscription.

A person may carry arms while entering, crossing, or leaving a sub-jurisdiction, on the roads and paths that make that travel possible, and may keep them in a vehicle and at home. No rule requires the arms to be surrendered as a condition of passage or exit.

A jail or a courtroom, national or local, may forbid bearing arms inside that building only while armed guards staff a gun-detection booth at each entrance the public uses. The booth is where arms are detected before a person goes further, and the guards are armed while they do that work. A sign, an unarmed clerk, or a lockbox is not this booth. Officers at a staffed booth may require the arms to be left outside the building. A penalty for going past the booth while armed exists only when a statute or local statute states it, and only while the booth is staffed as this section requires. A ban without that booth and those guards is void. The exception does not cover roads, borders of the town, or homes.

The owner of private property may forbid arms there. Concealed carrying on that property is not, by itself, a crime. When the owner discovers the arms and asks the person to leave, refusal is trespass. If the owner staffs the same armed detection booth at the entrance, the owner may refuse entry to a person who will not leave the arms at that booth, and passing the booth against that refusal is trespass. In either case the offense is trespass, not the keeping or bearing of arms.

Using arms to attack a person or property is not an exercise of this right. It is an ordinary crime or civil wrong.

## Privacy

A person has the right to be secure in person, home, papers, devices, communications, and private affairs. The [privacy](privacy.md) article states the operational rules. A charter may demand only the disclosures that article allows.

## Silence

A person has the right to remain silent, and the right not to be compelled to supply the evidence that convicts that person. The right covers testimony, private papers, financial records, a password, a biometric used as a key to the person's own mind or accounts, and any compulsion to decrypt. The prosecution proves its charge without forcing the accused to prove it. A warrant may authorize seizure of a device only as the privacy article allows. It cannot compel the person to open it.

No adverse inference of guilt is drawn from silence in a criminal case.

## Property and contract

A person may acquire, use, and dispose of property, and may make contracts.

A later statute or charter does not rewrite a contract already made. It may govern future contracts.

Property is taken only for a public use, only by judicial process, and only after compensation is paid. A tax enacted by the rule that governs that tax is not a taking. A regulation that leaves a parcel with no lawful use is a taking.

The national land tax is secured only by the land. Personal property is not the nation's security for that tax.

## Livelihood

Lawful work and lawful enterprise are not crimes. The nation issues no occupational license and grants no monopoly.

A sub-jurisdiction may license an activity only to reduce a concrete danger of physical harm to other people. It may not use a license to reserve a trade to incumbents. No license is required for speech, worship, or a parent's education of that parent's own child.

Pursuit of wealth through lawful exchange is not a crime and is not punished as such. A local tax enacted openly under the federalism article is a tax, not a punishment.

## Due process

No person is deprived of life, liberty, or property except by the law in force at the time and by a fair proceeding.

Criminal process includes:

- a presumption of innocence, with the burden on the prosecution
- a public charge that states the act and an offense already enacted
- the right to be silent, to refuse decryption, and to refuse to produce the records that would convict the accused
- proof by evidence the prosecution obtains without compelling that production
- the right to counsel, provided at public expense when incarceration is possible and the accused wants counsel
- a jury, as the [judiciary](judiciary.md) article requires, for any charge that can incarcerate
- confrontation of witnesses, and compulsory process for witnesses in the person's favor
- a public trial, closed only to the extent needed to protect a minor victim's identity, with the judgment still public
- a speedy trial: an accused held in custody is released from custody if trial has not begun within 180 days, unless that accused extends the time
- habeas corpus, available from a national court against any jail in the nation
- no ex post facto criminal law, and no retroactive tax
- no bill of attainder, including no law that names a person or a closed list of persons for punishment or for a special tax
- no second trial, in any court of the nation, for the same act after an acquittal or a conviction
- no cruel or disproportionate punishment

Civil forfeiture of property, without a criminal conviction of the owner for the act that justifies the forfeiture, does not exist. The land-tax auction is not forfeiture. It is a sale for a tax secured by that land, under the taxation statute.

## Parents and children

Parents direct the upbringing, education, and ordinary medical care of their minor children.

Any level of government may act, through a court, to stop violence, sexual abuse, or an imminent threat of death or serious permanent injury. Disagreement with a parent's religion, speech, or schooling is not that threshold. Nor is a medical decision that is not an imminent threat of death or serious permanent injury.

Home education and private education are lawful everywhere. No level of government treats them as inferior to a government school, or prohibits them.

A guest citizen is not a second voter for the guardian.

## Travel and exit

A person may travel inside the nation, may leave any sub-jurisdiction, and may leave the nation, with all personal property, including arms, at any time.

No exit tax exists. No official detains a person for leaving, or seizes personal property at the border because the person is leaving. A debt or a tax may still be sued on, and land that stays behind may still be liened, under the [federalism](federalism.md) article.

Citizens may enter the nation. That right is stated again in the defense article.

## Equal application

In rights, punishments, and eligibility for a public trust, the law does not classify persons by race, sex, creed, or political affiliation. The officer rule in the religion section is the exception, and it reaches only a charter's own officers.

Geography may change a land-tax rate where the cost of protection differs, under the money article, and nowhere else as a burden on persons.

A law is written in general terms. A law aimed at a named faction is an attainder.

## Status is not a crime

Poverty, debt, unemployment, and the lack of a home are not crimes at any level. Trespass and destruction of property remain civil wrongs and may be local crimes. A person may sleep and be present in a place where that person has a right to be. A sub-jurisdiction may offer shelter. It may prohibit camping on land the person has no right to use. It does not jail a person for the status of being poor.

## What is not a right

The following are not rights against the nation, and they do not become rights because a sub-jurisdiction provides them:

- health care
- housing
- income
- food
- education

A sub-jurisdiction may offer any of them as a program, funded by a tax its own people have accepted under the federalism article, on equal terms where this constitution requires equal terms. Ending the program for the future violates no right.
