# Privacy

Privacy is a national guarantee. Sub-jurisdictions may be thicker governments. They may not become surveillance states. The rights article states the right. This article states the rules an officer, a court, and a charter actually follow.

## Collection

An officer collects information about a person only when a statute or a charter in force names the datum and the purpose, and only for that purpose. The record states how long the datum is kept, and it is deleted at the end of that period.

Bulk collection of communications, locations, reading habits, associations, or financial life is outside every level of government. A program that collects first and justifies later is unlawful.

The third-party habit is rejected. Information a person shared with a bank, a carrier, a platform, a doctor, or an employer keeps its protection against the state. For a criminal investigation, a charge and a warrant are still required.

## Warrants

An officer may enter a place to stop violence that is happening then. That entry is not a search for other crimes. Evidence of the violence then underway may be used for that event.

Any other search or seizure of a person's body, home, devices, papers, or finances, and any compelled disclosure of them for a criminal investigation, waits until a charge against that person exists. A national or local judge then issues a warrant naming the person or place, the things sought, and that charge. General warrants are void. A local judge does not issue a warrant that commands a national officer.

Evidence taken under the warrant is used only to support that charge. It is not used to discover a different offense or to bring a different charge. Evidence of a different offense, seen during the search, is not the basis of a new charge.

A person may consent to a search of that person's own property. Consent is not a charge. Opening a land commitment for a buyer, a boundary dispute, a tax auction, or a judgment creditor follows the land section below. That opening is not a criminal search.

A warrant may authorize seizure of a device. It does not compel a password or decryption. The right to silence covers both.

Notice is given to the person when the search happens. After the charge exists, a judge may delay notice only while an immediate notice would let that person destroy the things the warrant names. The delay ends when that reason ends. The delayed notice and the warrant become part of the query log. There is no search of this kind before the charge, and so no secret search that the charge has not yet justified.

## The query log

Every official query for a person's information is written to an append-only log: who asked, the warrant or the statutory assessment authority, and the time. The log is public except for the parts a delay covers, and those parts become public when the delay ends. An officer who queries outside this rule commits a national crime.

## Land, tax, and identity

Parcel boundaries, the assessment, the rate, the formula inputs, and the existence of a lien are public. Markets and neighbors need them.

The identity of an owner, the identity of a legal entity's principal, and the history of that owner's payments are commitments. They are opened to a named party by a warrant or by the owner's consent: a buyer, a neighbor in a boundary dispute, a court conducting a tax auction, or a judgment creditor of a local tax. They are not a public roll of who owns the country.

A tax-payment proof shows that a named parcel's assessment is paid. It does not reveal the payer's other balances or other transactions.

A sub-jurisdiction that levies a lawful tax may require the disclosures strictly necessary to compute that tax on an act performed inside its territory, or on property situated there. The disclosure is used for that assessment only, kept only through the appeal period, and then deleted. It is not a statement of net worth and not a catalog of accounts. A tax that cannot be assessed without that statement or that catalog is not levied. The disclosure is not copied into a general dossier, is not demanded from a person who is not that tax's taxpayer, and is not used to discover or bring a criminal charge.

## Cameras and movement

The nation does not run, fund, or accept the product of a system that identifies a person in a public place and keeps a record of where that person goes. Neither does a combination of sub-jurisdictions.

A sub-jurisdiction may record a specific public building or a toll gate it owns, for the security of that place or for collecting that toll. The recording is not converted into a record of identified persons' movements. It is deleted within 30 days unless a warrant, issued after a charge for that incident, keeps that incident's recording for that charge alone.

No government shares a recording, or an identity derived from one, with another government in order to build such a movement record, or in a form that makes one possible. A database stitched from town cameras is a national catalog. Building it is unlawful even when every camera was installed locally. Travelers and neighbors did not join that town's experiment by passing a lens.

## Proofs and credentials

A citizen proves a single fact with a fresh proof. Proofs are unlinkable across verifiers unless the citizen links them. The enrollment set proves uniqueness without a readable list of persons, as the citizenship article requires.

The nation does not build a second identity system for "convenience."

## Agents

An agent is a tool of a person or a legal entity. It is not a reason to collect a catalog of tools, and it is not a person whose "privacy" displaces the principal's responsibility. A person who uses an agent does not thereby waive the warrant rule for that person's home, devices, or accounts.

## Offices of force

Office-holders are public because they wield force. Their biometrics are not a public record and not a retained record. The ceremony that qualifies a citizen destroys the sample in the same way as enrollment.

## Remedies

A person whose information was taken or kept outside this article may sue the officer personally and may sue to have the datum deleted. A statute or charter rule that orders bulk collection or a public identity catalog is void.
