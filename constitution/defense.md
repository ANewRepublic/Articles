# Defense and treaties

The nation defends its territory and the lives and property of people in it. It does not use the defense force to spread a way of life, to police foreign nations, or to police its own citizens in ordinary life.

## The force

The defense force is composed of volunteers. No level of government conscripts a person into military service. An organized militia of volunteers is lawful. Bearing arms under the rights article does not enroll anyone in a force.

The council commands the force within this article. It may hire a commander from the volunteers for a fixed term no longer than two years. The commander does not make policy and is dismissed when five council members say so, or when the term ends.

The force does not enforce local criminal law and does not act as a domestic police. It acts inside the borders to repel an attack, to restore national functions after an attack, and to carry out a judgment that a sub-jurisdiction has taken up arms against the nation.

Funding is current receipts. There is no war debt.

## Hostilities

An armed attack on the territory is met at once under the executive article.

Any other use of force outside that defensive authority, including an expedition beyond the borders, requires a grave ballot. The authorization states the opponent and the aim, lasts no longer than 12 months, and expires unless a new grave ballot renews it. There is no standing authorization.

Operational orders during an authorization are not laws and die with the authorization.

## Treaties

A treaty is limited to peace, trade, or alliance. Its text is a proposal. It becomes law only by an ordinary ballot, and it sunsets within the sunset limit unless enacted again.

A treaty does not change domestic law except by words that have themselves been enacted as national text. There is no self-executing treaty and no secret annex. If the other party wants different words, those words are a new proposal.

An alliance does not start hostilities. Hostilities still require the grave ballot, or the defensive authority when the territory is attacked.

The council transmits the enacted text. It does not sign a different bargain.

## Supply, imports, and exports

A land tax on farms can raise the price of domestic food. In trade with other nations, buyers may then import food instead, and a neighbor can threaten to cut that food off. The same pattern holds for other necessities and for military material. The nation may answer that risk. It may not use the answer as ordinary protection of an industry or as a second tax system.

A statute enacted by a grave ballot may:

- lay a tariff on the import of a named good, or
- prohibit or condition the import or export of a named good

The statute states the good, the concrete way a foreign power could use denial or acquisition of that good to coerce the nation, who enforces the control, and what enforcement costs. Food for the people in the nation qualifies when the statute shows a real dependency. So does material the defense force would lack in an attack. A preference for a domestic producer, standing alone, does not.

The control binds at the national border. It does not apply to goods moving between places inside the nation. Receipts from a tariff go to the treasury and do not justify the tariff. A court applies the control's own words: the named good, the border, and no rule on movement inside the nation. The court does not retry the motive of the ballot. The control ends at its sunset unless a new grave ballot renews it. The council enforces only the controls then in force. It does not invent a customs policy of its own.

## Entry

Citizens enter and remain by right.

Other persons enter and remain as a statute provides. Until a statute says otherwise, the council may admit or refuse a non-citizen and may require a non-citizen to leave after notice and a reasonable time to depart. A person who claims citizenship is entitled to a hearing in a national court before removal, and remains until the court decides.

Removal of a non-citizen is individual. There is no collective expulsion and no sale of admission that skips the citizenship oath. A person who is present, lawfully or not, keeps the rights of a person until a lawful removal. Unlawful presence is not, by itself, a national crime unless a statute makes a specific fraudulent entry a crime within the criminal article's limits. The founding text does not.

## Treason

Treason is levying war against the nation, or adhering to its enemies by giving them aid and comfort during hostilities. It requires an overt act proved by two witnesses to the same act, or a confession in open court.

Dissent, draft proposals, harsh speech, and voting against a war are not treason.

## Intelligence

The nation does not run bulk surveillance of people inside the borders. Collection aimed at foreign enemies does not become a backdoor into the communications of people here. A search of a person's communications follows the privacy article, and it waits for a public charge. A program that cannot name its targets that way is unlawful, including when it is called foreign.
