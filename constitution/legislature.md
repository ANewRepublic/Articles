# Legislature

The legislature is the eligible weight of the jurisdiction, voting on a frozen text. There are no legislative offices, no committees, and no capital the legislature sits in. Proceedings are the repository.

Anyone may draft. An agent may draft and may attach an analysis. A sponsor and every co-sponsor is a citizen of that jurisdiction. An agent does not sponsor, co-sponsor, or vote.

A national proposal uses national weight. A local proposal uses the weight of that sub-jurisdiction. The steps below are the same in both.

## What a proposal contains

A proposal is a change to one file, which is its subject, and it includes:

- the kind: constitution, statute, charter, or local statute
- the subject, matching that file
- the authority: the constitutional sentence that allows it, for anything that is not itself a constitutional change
- a sunset date within the sunset limit, for a statute, a local statute, or a local tax authorization
- whether it claims to be enduring, which only the list below may be
- a spending figure, if it spends
- the hash of the frozen text

The diff is the law. A summary that disagrees with the diff enacts nothing.

A proposal that creates a sub-jurisdiction may contain a full charter over the word cap, and is a constitutional ballot of the residents of that land. Every later amendment obeys the word cap.

## The only checks that can stop a review

Review starts when ballot access is met and the following are true. No other check exists. An officer does not waive one and does not add one. A citizen may obtain a writ to start the clock when they are met.

1. The fields above are present and match the diff.
2. The proposal edits one file and one subject.
3. The normative text is within the word cap, or is a founding charter.
4. Co-sponsors meet ballot access.
5. The same normative text is not in a failed proposal whose reintroduction wait is still running. The wait ends when one decision day has passed. A renewal of a statute still in force is not a failed proposal.
6. A spending proposal is within receipts on hand plus the unspent reserve, measured against treasury figures any citizen can recompute from the governance record.
7. A statute does not edit the constitution. A local proposal does not edit national text.
8. The enduring label is used only for an enduring statute. A proposal that edits one keeps the label. Dropping a statute from the enduring list is a constitutional change, not a field in the proposal.
9. A proposal that forbids, compels, taxes, or punishes states, in plain words, what conduct is covered, who enforces it, and what enforcement costs. The cost is funded as spending under check 6. The attachment of that plan is a mechanical check. Whether an ordinary adult can understand the rule, and whether the plan can be carried out without breaking a guarantee, are decided by a court under the criminal article.

A spending cut or a repeal needs no spending certificate and no enforcement plan.

## Review

The text is frozen when review starts. A change in wording is a new proposal.

Review lasts the review period. During it, any citizen may attach one statement a day to the proposal, at no charge, either under a name they choose or as a unique citizen with an unlinkable proof. No official deletes a statement for its viewpoint. A court may remove a statement that is itself a national crime.

A hosted forum may charge for rank and may set house rules, because it is not the record. The free statements are the record.

Any person or agent may attach an analysis of how the diff interacts with existing law. The nation designates no official model. A mechanical note may list other articles that define the same terms. Silence from that note means nothing. An analysis never blocks enactment and never becomes law. Only the checks above, an objection threshold, or a ballot does.

## The consent calendar

The consent calendar is only for proposals that do not grow the government's power. It may carry:

- a repeal of an ordinary statute, a local statute, or a local tax
- a spending cut or a fee cut
- a new ordinary statute that does not create or widen a crime, a confinement, a tax, a fee, a toll, or a spending program

It does not carry constitutional text, an enduring statute, a treaty, a renewal, or anything the next section puts on a grave or constitutional ballot.

If a proposal is allowed on this path and objections stay under the objection threshold through the end of review, it merges on the next monthly fifteenth.

A citizen objects with that citizen's own weight, and may do so until the close. A delegate's objection counts only after the delegate notice, and only for weight whose delegator has not withdrawn it or voted directly. A direct objection counts even when the delegate would rather let the proposal pass.

Silence on this path is acceptance. The 90 days, the public diff, and a minority large enough to object are the safeguards. Silence is not acceptance of a new crime, a new tax, or a treaty.

If objections reach the threshold, the proposal does not merge. It goes to the next decision day as an ordinary ballot, unless the constitution requires a grave or constitutional ballot for that subject.

## Ballots

Decision days carry the ballots that need an affirmative vote:

- an ordinary ballot for an ordinary statute that is not on the consent calendar, for a renewal, for a treaty, for a lower criminal maximum, and for a local repeal that drew an objection
- a grave ballot to commence hostilities, to extend a civil emergency past the council's own authority, to dissolve a sub-jurisdiction after armed resistance, to repeal an enduring statute, to add or remove a national offense, to raise a maximum sentence, to set the land-tax rate at zero, or to lay or renew a security tariff or an import or export control
- a constitutional ballot to change this constitution, to add a local power or a local tax, or to remove a judge or the prosecutor for inability

On an enduring file, an identical renewal, a lower criminal maximum, and a land-tax rate that stays above zero use an ordinary ballot. Every other change to an enduring file uses a grave ballot. A proposal that mixes a grave change with any other change uses a grave ballot.

A constitutional ballot that passes on a decision day is voted again on the next decision day. The text stays frozen between them. Failure on either day rejects it. One review period before the first of those days is enough.

The vote is yes or no on the frozen hash. There is no third option and no amendment on the ballot.

Repeal of ordinary text may use the consent calendar. Repeal of an enduring statute, and adding or removing a national offense, takes a grave ballot, so the criminal list is not undone by silence or by a thin ordinary vote. Repeal of a constitutional rule is a constitutional change. A lower maximum sentence is an ordinary ballot. Repeal is otherwise held to no higher bar than enactment.

A failed proposal leaves the law as it was.

## Enactment

A passed proposal is merged to the staging branch by a threshold signature over the tally and the commit id. It becomes the enforced branch on the enactment day: the monthly fifteenth for a consent proposal, or the moment of finality of a decision-day tally.

The signature is a ministerial act. The council has no veto. Delay is cured by a writ, and any citizen may rely on the governance record's commit id if a mirror lags.

The law takes effect at enactment unless the text names a later moment. A crime or a tax applies only to acts and periods after it takes effect.

The council, a host, or a ledger operator who refuses to publish an enacted commit, or who publishes a different one, commits a national crime.

## Sunset

Every statute, local statute, and local tax authorization names a sunset within the sunset limit.

A renewal is an ordinary ballot of the same text, or of a new text that itself passes. The consent calendar cannot renew a law. For ordinary text, filing the renewal is not enough. If no renewal has been enacted by the sunset date, the text leaves the enforced branch. Inattention repeals it. Stale rules and stale spending are how a small government grows back.

A renewal is a new enactment. It ends every judicial precedent tied to the previous enactment, even when the words are unchanged. An interpretation continues only when the renewed text states it. The judiciary article is the rest of that rule.

An enduring statute stays in force when its renewal fails for any reason, including a no vote. A failed renewal does not legalize murder, abolish the land tax, or stop the count of votes. It does end precedent tied to the cycle that closed. Repeal is a separate grave ballot. The council introduces a renewal at least 180 days before sunset. The enduring list is closed:

- the national criminal statute
- the incarceration statute
- the land-tax formula
- the cryptographic specification

Labeling any other file as enduring is a constitutional change. It is not a field a sponsor may tick.

## What this article does not do

Emergencies, battlefield orders, and judgments are not proposals and do not amend the enforced branch. They expire under their own articles. No court holds a key that merges or deletes law. A judgment does not remove text and does not substitute wording.
