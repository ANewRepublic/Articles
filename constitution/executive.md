# Executive

The executive is a council of seven citizens. It carries out enacted law. It does not write law, waive law, or choose which statutes are real.

There is no president. Foreign states negotiate with the enacted text. The council may name a clerk to speak for it. The clerk binds the nation to nothing.

## Selection

Members are drawn by lot from citizens with national voting weight. The draw is public. Its seed is committed on the governance record before the pool is closed, so the council cannot steer the lot.

The term is one year. Seats are staggered. A member is not drawn again for six years after a term ends.

Service is a duty, like jury duty, because the people who should do it will not campaign for it. A member serves from home. The nation does not require a move to a capital. There is no capital.

A hardship exemption is decided by a panel of six citizens drawn by lot, not by the council: illness, primary care of a dependent, a livelihood that would be ruined by the absence, or recent service. An unexcused refusal pauses the citizen's voting weight for the year they would have served. Refusal is not a crime and not a debt.

Pay is a per diem set by ordinary statute to cover the cost of leaving ordinary work for the days actually required. It is not a prize, a pension, or a reason to seek the office. When the pay statute sunsets, members still serve and the per diem falls back to a published daily amount equal to the last enacted per diem until a new statute passes. That fallback exists so a failed calendar cannot empty the council. It does not grow.

Delegations to a sitting member are suspended.

The founding rule for an initial council is in the introduction. Initial members have the duties in this article and no more.

## What the council does

The council:

- collects the national land tax under the formula in force
- runs enrollment ceremonies
- carries out final judgments
- commands the defense force within the defense article
- publishes treasury figures that any citizen can recompute
- publishes the frontier proposal, if it proposes one, as a draft statute and not as a rule
- admits or refuses non-citizens under the defense article
- hires, for the duties in this list only

Employees are hired for no longer than two years and for no longer than the remaining need of the duty. There is no tenure. There are no agencies with the power to write binding rules. If a job is not in the list above, the council does not hire for it.

The council acts by the agreement of at least four members, except where this article requires five. Vacancies do not lower those numbers. If fewer than four members remain, a draw fills the empty seats before the council acts further, except for defense during the first 72 hours of an armed attack. Acts are public, signed, and dated. An unpublished instruction is not an act of the council.

## What the council does not do

The council does not:

- enact, amend, or veto a proposal
- create a duty, a crime, a tax, a license, or a spending program
- spend money a statute has not already authorized, or spend beyond receipts on hand plus the unspent reserve
- adopt a policy of declining to enforce a statute
- direct the prosecutor to charge or to spare any person
- suspend a right, even in an emergency
- negotiate a secret treaty or sign a text the legislature has not already enacted

Prosecutorial discretion over a single case, for insufficient evidence, belongs to the prosecutor and not to the council. A policy of never enforcing a named statute is a refusal to execute the law. A citizen may obtain a writ of mandate to compel a ministerial act, including publication of an enacted commit, collection under the tax formula, and destruction of enrollment samples.

## Civil emergency

When a disaster makes an enumerated duty impossible to perform as written, five members may declare a civil emergency for 30 days and may extend it once for 30 days more.

During that period the council may spend from the reserve for the disaster and may give operational orders to employees and to the defense force for rescue and for the protection of life and property. It may not create crimes, suspend rights, censor speech, disarm the people, or conduct bulk surveillance.

A further extension is a grave ballot, for at most 90 days at a time. The decree never merges into the enforced branch. When the period ends, the decree is gone.

## Armed attack

An armed attack on the territory is not a civil emergency and does not wait for a vote. The council directs the defense of the territory while the attack continues and for 30 days after it ends. Four members direct that defense. During the first 72 hours, the members who can be reached may direct it, and they record the orders as soon as the record can be written. That lower number applies only to defensive orders while an attack is underway. It is not a way to start an expedition. The authority covers defensive operations and the restoration of the national functions named in this constitution. It adds no domestic power. Hostilities beyond defense of the territory require the grave ballot in the defense article.

## The reserve

The reserve exists for civil emergencies and for the short period at the start of an armed attack. It cannot exceed the reserve cap. It is filled only by receipts already collected. It is not borrowed.
