# Sub-jurisdictions

The nation stays small so people who disagree can share a country. Who sits on the council matters little, because the council cannot reach a local experiment. A group of people on contiguous land may form a sub-jurisdiction and try a thicker government: subsidize a thing, serve a group, and tax something the nation itself is forbidden to tax. They may not break the guarantees in this constitution. Those guarantees are what make the experiment peaceful, and what make leaving it real.

Land with no charter is a lawful way to live. The nation does not require anyone to join a thicker government. On unorganized land, national law is the whole government.

Two charters may contradict each other. That is the design working, when both honor the guarantees. A national officer does not use the office to pick the winning experiment. A court decides, in the case before it, whether a guarantee was broken. It does not decide which experiment is wiser, and it does not delete the experiment's text.

## What a charter may do

A charter may:

- offer services and subsidies
- tax acts performed inside the territory, and property situated inside it, to pay for what the charter states, including income, sales, personal property, and transactions, which the nation may not levy
- fund schools and libraries on the equal terms stated below
- keep peace officers for its own law
- adopt local statutes, including local crimes within the [criminal](criminal.md) article
- set rules for future employment inside its territory
- set rules for roads, conduits, and other property the sub-jurisdiction itself owns
- choose local officers by election, by lot, or by hire

A program creates no right to its continuation. Sums already due for a period already completed are paid. The future is repealable.

## What a charter may not do

A charter and its officers do not:

- narrow a [right](rights.md)
- impose an exit tax, detain a person for leaving, or seize personal property because the person is leaving
- stop a person from passing, or trap a resident who wants to depart, including by disarming them on the way
- conscript anyone into military service
- issue money, or make any instrument legal tender for private debts
- borrow, guarantee a debt, or spend beyond receipts already in hand
- ask for or pressure a civic oath, or establish a church, or compel support of one
- apply a religious test to anyone except its own officers, and then only as a requirement of membership in a stated religion
- treat a government school as preferred to a private school or to home education
- deny one vote to a resident citizen, give any person more than one local vote, or give a local vote to a non-citizen
- punish an act performed outside the territory, or write a penalty or a tax that turns on what another place permits or forbids
- levy a wealth tax, or tax a bank balance or a financial holding
- write secret law, or make law by executive order
- amend this constitution or a national statute
- keep an identity catalog, or run bulk surveillance, beyond the [privacy](privacy.md) article
- compel a medical or surgical procedure
- expect the nation to pay the sub-jurisdiction's bills

A local tax is not a national tax. A charter may levy taxes the nation may not levy, and it may not levy a wealth tax at all. The money article states that limit. A local tax is prospective. It is enacted only by the ballot named below. It reaches an act performed inside the territory, or property that sits inside the territory. Income from labor or a sale inside the territory may be taxed when the charter says so. A wage earned outside, a purchase made outside, and a balance held at a bank are not that town's base. Land inside the territory may be taxed because the land is inside. After a person has in fact changed primary dwelling to a place outside the sub-jurisdiction, later acts outside are not that sub-jurisdiction's to tax. Taxes already accrued on inside acts, and taxes already accrued on land that remains, stay owing. The remedies are a civil suit and a lien on land that remains inside the territory. The remedy is not jail, and it is not a stop at the border.

## Forming, nesting, and leaving a territory

A sub-jurisdiction begins by consent, on land its founders own. At least 21 adult citizens, owning the included parcels among them, propose a charter. Every parcel is included only with the consent of all its owners. The resident citizens of those parcels then adopt the charter by a constitutional ballot. One person who owns many parcels is still one founder.

The territory is one connected piece. It does not surround a parcel whose owner has not joined. Neighbors who want that parcel buy it at a price the owner accepts. They do not encircle it, and they do not vote it in.

A parcel is added later only the same way: every owner's consent, a connected result, and no new ring around an unwilling parcel. The town already in existence does not annex by its own majority.

An enclosing jurisdiction may object to a formation or a growth only by a case in a national court, and only on the ground that the new boundary would leave some of its residents without a route that defense or emergency aid can use without a serious delay. The objection fails if the proposers record a right of passage sufficient for that aid. The court decides. The enclosing jurisdiction's political dislike of the new charter is not a ground, and its council does not hold a veto.

An inner sub-jurisdiction lies entirely inside an outer one. Sibling territories do not overlap. A citizen's enclosing jurisdictions are the nation and each territory that contains the primary dwelling.

A border parcel leaves a sub-jurisdiction when every owner asks and the territory that remains would stay connected, without cutting the enclosing jurisdiction's recorded passage. If resident citizens live on that parcel, they also pass an ordinary ballot to leave. Personal departure does not require a vote. The person leaves when the primary dwelling actually changes.

Dissolving a whole sub-jurisdiction is a constitutional ballot of the citizens who hold its vote. A national court applies this constitution in the case before it when a charter's words cannot be obeyed together with a national guarantee. It does not remove the charter from the repository. The nation dissolves a sub-jurisdiction only after a judgment that it has taken up arms against the nation, followed by a national grave ballot.

## Local law and local officers

Local law lives in a public git repository with the same mechanics as the [legislature](legislature.md) article, scaled to that eligible weight. The text is searchable in plain words, without an account. The national constitution is a read-only input. A local enactment whose words cannot be obeyed together with a national guarantee is handled as the judiciary article provides. The text stays until that sub-jurisdiction changes it. Secret local law is void from the start.

Adding a local power or a local tax is a constitutional ballot of that sub-jurisdiction. Repealing a local power, tax, or statute is an ordinary ballot or the consent calendar, so an experiment can end more easily than it began. Local statutes and local tax authorizations carry a sunset no later than the sunset limit. Renewing one is an ordinary proposal.

Officers carry out enacted local law. They do not write it, suspend it, or speak for the nation. A local "emergency" cannot exceed the national limits on emergencies and cannot touch a national right. Who the local officer is should matter less than what the charter already says.

A sub-jurisdiction may run its own courts for its own law. A person who claims a national guarantee can take that question to a national court. The national judgment controls.

## Education

The nation never spends on schools or libraries.

A sub-jurisdiction may fund education, higher or lower, and libraries, only on terms a family may use equally for a government school, a private school, or education at home. A rule that treats one of those as the normal path and the others as a lesser path is void.

## Taxes, liens, and bailouts

National and local taxes may stack on the same parcel. The national land-tax lien is senior. Local liens rank in the order the governance record received them.

The nation never returns tax money to a sub-jurisdiction on the condition that it adopt a policy, and never assumes or guarantees a local obligation. Local capital works are paid from receipts already in hand. Debt is bondage at every level, including for a city that is sure its project is wise.

## Peace between experiments

A sub-jurisdiction enforces its own law with its own officers. National officers enforce national judgments and national crimes. A local court does not order those officers to stop or to proceed. A local officer arrests for a national crime in progress, or under a national warrant, and delivers the person to the national court without adding a local charge for the same act.

A charter cannot legalize damage to a person or to property whose owner has not agreed. An owner who loses that suit in a local forum may bring the claim in a national court.
