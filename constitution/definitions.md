# Definitions

These meanings control every article that uses the word. A change to this article is a constitutional change.

## People

**Person.** A living human being who has been born. National citizenship, national voting, and national crimes use this meaning. It does not settle the status of human life before birth. A sub-jurisdiction may protect that life further. Extending this definition is a constitutional change.

**Adult.** A person at least 18 years of age.

**Minor.** A person under 18 years of age.

**Citizen.** An adult who holds current citizenship under the [citizenship](citizenship.md) article.

**Guest citizen.** A minor with at least one guardian who is a citizen. A guest citizen is not a citizen, does not vote, and cannot delegate.

**Primary dwelling.** The place a citizen actually sleeps on a majority of nights during the twelve months before a renewal or a declared change. A citizen may declare a change at most once every 180 days. A change counts for a ballot only when the declaration is at least 90 days old.

**Resident.** A citizen whose primary dwelling is in the place named. A person is a resident of the nation, and of each enclosing sub-jurisdiction, or of none.

**Enclosing jurisdiction.** The nation, and each sub-jurisdiction whose territory contains the citizen's primary dwelling.

## Votes

**Voting weight.** One vote. A citizen has at most one vote in a jurisdiction. National weight follows the citizenship article. In a sub-jurisdiction, each resident citizen has one vote, and any other citizen has one vote only when the charter extends it on a general condition. A specific article may pause weight.

**Eligible weight.** The voting weight entitled to be counted on a proposal. It includes only citizens who already held that weight in that jurisdiction when the proposal's review began. It is recounted at the close to drop weight that has since ended.

**Weight cast.** Eligible weight voted yes or no. An abstention is not weight cast.

**Participation.** Weight cast divided by eligible weight.

**Delegate.** A citizen who casts another citizen's voting weight under the [representation](representation.md) article.

**Delegate notice.** Seven days. A delegate's vote or objection counts only after the delegator has been able to see it for this long, and only if the delegator has not overridden it.

**Public delegate.** A delegate who holds at least 50 citizens' voting weight and at least 1 percent of that jurisdiction's eligible weight. Weight is measured when review starts and again at the close. Crossing the threshold at either time makes the delegate public for that proposal.

**Super-majority.** The yes share of a constitutional ballot: at least 80 percent of weight cast. A super-majority passes nothing by itself. The constitutional ballot also requires its participation floor and a second passage.

## Ballots

Ballots of a jurisdiction use that jurisdiction's eligible weight. A citizen may hold weight in the nation and in a nested sub-jurisdiction at the same time, and casts or delegates each separately.

**Review period.** 90 days. The proposal's text is frozen when the period starts.

**Objection threshold.** Objections carrying 5 percent of eligible weight.

**Consent calendar.** Passage by the end of review when objections have stayed under the objection threshold. Silence is acceptance on this path only.

**Ordinary ballot.** More yes than no, and participation of at least 20 percent. Silence is abstention. Abstention can fail the floor.

**Grave ballot.** Yes votes of at least 60 percent of weight cast, and participation of at least 30 percent.

**Constitutional ballot.** A super-majority, and participation of at least 50 percent, on two consecutive decision days, with identical text.

**Decision day.** 15 January, 15 April, 15 July, and 15 October.

**Monthly fifteenth.** The 15th day of each month.

**Ballot access.** Co-sponsors equal to one-tenth of the jurisdiction's eligible weight when review is requested, rounded up, never more than 50, and never fewer than 1.

**Word cap.** 800 words of normative text in a single proposal. A charter that founds a sub-jurisdiction may be longer, because its guarantees have to exist together.

**Sunset limit.** 8 years.

## Laws and records

**Statute.** National law that is not this constitution.

**Charter.** The entrenched law of one sub-jurisdiction.

**Local statute.** Ordinary law under a charter.

**Constitutional change.** A change to this constitution, including these definitions. Adding a power or a tax to a charter is a constitutional change of that sub-jurisdiction, not of the nation.

**Enduring statute.** A statute on the closed list in the [legislature](legislature.md) article. It is debated again at each sunset. A failed renewal leaves it in force. Repealing it takes a grave ballot.

**Governance record.** The public append-only ledger identified by the cryptographic specification. It stores commitments, tallies, and the commit id of the law. It does not store the prose of the law, and its operators are not a legislature.

**Enforced branch.** The git branch whose current commit is law.

**Staging branch.** The git branch that holds text approved and not yet in force.

**Enactment.** The moment a commit becomes the enforced branch.

## Land, money, and tools

**Land.** A parcel of ground and the rights in it that are not improvements.

**Improvement.** A building, crop, fixture, or other product of labor on a parcel.

**Unimproved value.** The market value of a parcel as if it had no improvements.

**Personal property.** Movable things, money, goods, and claims that are not land.

**Frontier rate.** A land-tax rate for parcels along the national border that cost more to defend. It is at most twice the ordinary rate.

**Legal entity.** An association registered so it may own land and pay the land tax. It is not a person and cannot vote. Every beneficial owner is a citizen. A living adult citizen is its principal and answers for it in court.

**Beneficial owner.** A person who ultimately controls an entity, or who owns any part of its economic benefit. Chains of entities are followed through to the persons at the end.

**National tie.** A dwelling in the nation that a citizen has a right to occupy, land in the nation that the citizen owns, or a business in the nation that the citizen operates.

**Agent.** A tool, including an autonomous system, that acts for a person or a legal entity. An agent is not a person, a citizen, a delegate, or a principal.

## Force

**Council.** The national executive council described in the [executive](executive.md) article.

**Charge.** A public accusation, filed in a court, that names the person, the act, and an offense a statute or local statute then in force already describes.

**National crime.** A crime named in the national [criminal statute](../statutes/crimes.md).

**Local cap.** Five years. The longest incarceration a sub-jurisdiction may impose for conduct that is not a national crime.

**Reserve cap.** The national land-tax receipts of the preceding year. The reserve cannot exceed it.
