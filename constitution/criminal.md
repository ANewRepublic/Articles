# Crime and punishment

No act is a crime unless a statute in force at the time names it. A court does not create a crime. An officer does not create a crime. A charter creates local crimes only as this article allows.

National crimes are a closed list in the [criminal statute](../statutes/crimes.md). Each one is force, fraud against the nation's own processes, a concrete danger to life or physical property, or an attack on the machinery of this constitution. Status, worship, speech within the rights article, and lawful enterprise are not national crimes.

## Purposes

Punishment exists to separate people who are dangerous, to make restitution to a victim, and to deter. Pain beyond those purposes is not a purpose. The surgical punishment for rape stated in the national criminal statute is part of separating a person convicted of that offense. It does not license any other bodily punishment. Rehabilitation may be offered. It is not a pretext for a longer confinement.

Sentences are proportionate to the harm. Every offense that can incarcerate states a minimum and a maximum. The maximum is a ceiling. The minimum is the floor. Neither is a suggestion that every case deserves an end of the range.

## Proof

A conviction requires admissible evidence on which an ordinary adult would settle, beyond a reasonable doubt, that the accused committed the charged offense. Suspicion is not that evidence. A plea is not that evidence. The same standard applies in a national court and in a local court.

## Pleas

The accused may plead guilty only to an offense already charged. The plea does not dismiss, reduce, or replace a charge. The prosecutor does not offer, and a court does not accept, a plea that drops a count, substitutes another offense, or sets a term other than the minimum stated for the offense charged.

Before a plea is accepted, the prosecutor files the evidence for the charge in the public record. A minor victim's identity is protected as the rights article allows. The judge accepts the plea only when that evidence already meets the proof section. If it does not, the plea is rejected. The case is then tried, or the prosecutor declines the charge.

A declination is a public statement that the evidence does not meet the proof section. It is not exchanged for a plea, for testimony, or for cooperation. Counts of declinations stay public as the judiciary article requires.

On an accepted plea, the term is the minimum. On a trial, the jury sets the term inside the range. A judge does not move either term below the minimum or above the maximum.

## Witnesses

No party, officer, or private person pays a witness for testimony. Payment includes a fee, a bonus, a gift, immunity, a dismissed or reduced charge, and a lower sentence. It includes a witness called for specialized knowledge. The ban binds the prosecution and the defense.

A witness may be paid the wages that witness loses for each day of required attendance, and nothing more. A witness who loses no wages is paid nothing. The party who calls the witness pays. Prosecution witnesses, and witnesses called by counsel appointed at public expense, are paid from land-tax receipts. Any other defense witness is paid by the defense. A claim for wages the witness did not lose is process corruption when the treasury pays it.

The nation may conceal a witness's location and may help the witness live elsewhere. That protection does not dismiss a charge, erase a conviction, or issue a credential, a name, or an identity that omits criminal history the person already has. The history stays on the credential and follows the person. A firearms disability stays.

## Clear and enforceable rules

A law that forbids, compels, taxes, or punishes has to be readable by an ordinary adult. The reader has to be able to tell what is required without guessing which people an officer will later choose. A law that fails that test is not enforced against the accused. The text stays on the enforced branch until the voters repeal it or it sunsets. A ruling to that effect may be cited only as the judiciary article allows.

A law that ordinary life routinely breaks, so that enforcement exists only by picking targets out of a crowd, is unenforceable for the same reason. A charge under a widely ignored rule, brought because of the accused's viewpoint or faction, is dismissed.

The proposal that creates the rule states who enforces it and what that costs, and the cost is funded, as the legislature article requires. A plan that works only by violating a national guarantee, including by searching homes for speech, reading, worship, or private possession, is not a lawful plan. The rule is not enforced. Charters are held to this section as statutes are.

## Who can be expected to know

The official copy of the law is the public text of the enforced branch. A person can search it in plain words without an account. The nation does not run a visitor center and does not publish an application. A sub-jurisdiction may keep a place where the text can be read, and may offer a search tool. The tool is a copy. The branch is the law.

A national crime applies to every person. Force, theft, fraud, and trespass are wrongs a traveler already knows. A local penalty for one of those wrongs applies without a further posting.

Any other local penalty applies to a person who is not a resident only when the text was on the enforced branch before the act and the rule is posted, in words an ordinary adult can read on the spot without a device, either where the act happens or at each public road entrance to the territory. A posting that an ordinary adult cannot take in while entering does not count. A resident is held to the sub-jurisdiction's published text. No person is held to a secret rule, or to a rule an ordinary adult cannot learn from that text.

A prosecution for a local rule the accused could not have learned under this section is dismissed.

## Acts outside the territory

A sub-jurisdiction does not punish a person for an act performed outside its territory. It does not matter whether that act was lawful where it was done. A local penalty is not written so that it turns on the law of some other place. The nation may still punish a national crime. The place where the act was performed may apply its own published law.

A person or a company outside a sub-jurisdiction is not subject to its penalties because someone inside received a communication or a virtual product from them. A physical good shipped into the territory arrives inside, and the sub-jurisdiction may regulate that arrival. A file, a message, a page, or another virtual product does not arrive in that sense. The recipient's residence, the recipient's location, and the recipient's hiding of that location do not move the sender inside. The sub-jurisdiction does not require the sender to detect the recipient's territory. A sender who is physically present and acting inside the territory is inside for that physical act.

## National civil wrongs

A person who uses force, commits fraud, or physically damages another person's body or property owes restitution. The victim may sue in a national court even when the act is not a national crime and even when a charter is silent or hostile. Pollution and other waste that causes that damage are within this sentence. A charter cannot legalize it.

The owner of the damaged property is the plaintiff. The suit is not a national planning scheme and not a license for a regulator.

## Local crimes

A sub-jurisdiction may punish conduct by incarceration only when all of the following are true:

- a local statute in force at the time names the act and states a minimum and a maximum
- the proceeding meets the rights article and the jury rules
- the term stays inside that range, does not exceed the local cap, and does not exceed the national maximum when the same act is also a national crime
- the act is not mere status, speech protected by the rights article, worship, or the fact of leaving the territory

A local crime that does not incarcerate may still be sued on for a civil penalty the charter states, collected without jail and without blocking exit.

Conduct that is a national crime is tried in a national court. A local arrest delivers the person to that court and adds no second charge for the same act. A local judgment on an act that is a national crime is void and is not a first trial. A town cannot use a lesser local charge, or a quick acquittal, to close a national case.

## Forfeiture, debt, and labor

Property is not forfeited because an officer suspects it. Forfeiture follows a conviction and is limited to the instruments and fruits of that crime, and it does not reach property of a person who was not convicted.

Debt, including unpaid tax, is not a reason for incarceration. The land-tax remedy is the land. A local tax remedy is a suit or a lien, not a cell.

Labor by a confined person is governed by the incarceration statute.

## Who is responsible for an agent

A person who directs an agent to commit an act is responsible for the act as if the person had done it. An agent is never the accused in place of that person, and an agent is never punished as though it were a person.
