# Judiciary

Courts decide cases under the text in force. They do not write statutes, invent positive rights, or complete a policy the legislature left unfinished. Where the text is silent, the national power does not exist. A local power exists only when the charter states it.

Pull-request discussion, forum arguments, and model-written explanations are not legislative history. They do not control the meaning of enacted text.

## Judges

A person enters the judicial pool by passing a published examination on this constitution and the statutes in force. The examination and a scoring key are public. Admission is ministerial when the score meets the published line. No association, officer, or council has a veto.

Judges are drawn by lot from that pool for a single term of eight years. There is no second judicial term and no promotion to a higher court as a prize. Cases are assigned by lot.

A judge may be removed for a criminal conviction, or for inability by a constitutional ballot. An unpopular judgment is not inability.

Judges serve from where they live. Pay is an ordinary statute. Delegations to a sitting judge are suspended.

The founding introduction governs the first judges.

## Prosecutor

The prosecutor is drawn by lot from a pool qualified by a separate published examination, for a single term of four years, and is not drawn again for eight years. The prosecutor is not under the council and is not removed by the council. Removal is a criminal conviction or a constitutional ballot for inability.

The prosecutor decides whether a particular case has evidence enough to charge. The prosecutor does not adopt a policy of never charging a national crime that remains in force. Counts of declinations, without the private facts of the files, are published each quarter.

Deputies, if any, serve no longer than the prosecutor's term. Their number and pay are an ordinary statute. If that statute lapses, the prosecutor continues without deputies.

Counsel for an accused facing incarceration is drawn by rotation from the advocate pool, not chosen by the prosecutor or the council, and is paid under statute from land-tax receipts. A person may always refuse counsel and speak for themselves. No license is required to speak in one's own case. Representing another person in a national court requires admission to the advocate pool by the open examination. There is no guild veto.

## Juries

A charge that can incarcerate is tried to a jury of 12 citizens of the jurisdiction whose law is being applied. Conviction requires all 12. The jury also sets the term, which cannot exceed the statutory maximum. A judge may lower a term that is grossly disproportionate and states the reasons. A judge does not raise a term.

A hung jury may be retried once. After that the charge is dismissed.

An acquittal is final. The prosecution does not appeal an acquittal or a sentence. The accused may appeal a conviction.

A juror is not punished for a verdict.

Jury service is a duty of citizens with voting weight in that jurisdiction, with the same hardship exemptions as council service, decided by a sortition panel. An unexcused refusal pauses voting weight for six months. The private voting terminal is available to a juror who is sequestered.

A civil claim for restitution is tried to a jury of 6 unless both sides agree to a judge. A judgment needs 5 of the 6.

A sub-jurisdiction may not convict a person to any incarceration on a smaller jury or by a less-than-unanimous vote. It may be more protective of the accused.

## What courts decide

National courts hear:

- national crimes
- civil claims of force, fraud, or physical damage to person or property, including when a local forum has refused the claim or has denied a national guarantee
- habeas corpus for any person held by any jail in the nation
- challenges to an assessment under the taxation statute
- writs of mandate to compel a ministerial act a statute already requires, such as publishing an enacted commit or destroying a biometric sample

A person sues on that person's own rights, property, or custody. An association may sue on its own contract or land. There is no action on behalf of the public at large, and no suit whose only aim is to strike a text from the repository.

A citizen who pays the national land tax may sue to stop a national expenditure that exceeds scope or exceeds receipts. The remedy is to stop the expenditure.

People harmed by the same act may join one case.

A panel of three national judges hears an appeal on the law and on whether any evidence could support the verdict. It does not retry the facts. At least two of the three agree, or the judgment below stands. Further review happens only when a panel certifies that two panels have stated contradictory readings of the same enacted text.

## What a judgment is

A judgment binds the parties to that case. It is not a statute and it is not an amendment.

The court holds no key to a repository. It does not merge, delete, or rewrite a commit. The threshold signature that enacted a text remains that text. The sign-off the legislature article requires is a tally of citizens. A court is not a signer.

One judge applies the law to the parties in the case. One judge does not overturn a statute, a charter, or a local statute for anyone else. Voters repair a bad text at sunset, by renewal, by repeal, or by the grave ballot.

Where the words of a statute, a charter, or a local statute and the words of this constitution cannot both be obeyed in the case, the court obeys this constitution for those parties. The other text stays on the enforced branch until the voters change it.

The court applies the words that were enacted. It does not try the motive of the ballot. It does not set a text aside because the stated reason is called a pretext.

If the enacted text does not cover the act, the accused is not convicted of it. The court does not supply the missing words.

A ruling names the statute, the charter sentence, the local statute, or the constitutional sentence it applies. A ruling tied to no enacted text is void.

## Precedent

A ruling may be cited as precedent only when all of the following are true:

- a national panel stated it unanimously
- the ruling names one enacted text other than this constitution
- the later case applies that same text, still unamended
- the citation falls during that text's current enactment

Sunset, repeal, and renewal end the precedent. Renewal ends it even when the new enactment uses the same words. On an enduring statute, the precedent also ends at the sunset date of that cycle. The text staying in force after a failed renewal is not a new enactment and does not carry the old citations forward. An interpretation continues only when a renewed text states it. That is how a judicial reading is ratified by the people, or ends.

A single judge's ruling is not precedent. A local court's ruling is not precedent. If two national panels state contradictory readings of the same text during one enactment, neither reading is precedent. Later courts read the text.

There is no binding precedent on the meaning of this constitution. Each court reads it. An opinion may persuade a later court. It does not bind one. Case law does not grow into a second constitution.

## What courts do not do

A court does not dictate an officer's discretion or set policy. A writ of mandate compels an act the enacted text already requires. It does not choose among lawful choices.

A local court does not order the national council, a national officer, or the execution of national law to stop or to proceed. An order that does so is void. Habeas corpus against a person held under national authority is a national writ.

Officers have no immunity for a violation of a national right or for a national crime. They answer personally. The treasury pays compensation for a taking the nation itself committed. The treasury does not pay punitive awards for an officer's own crime.

## Local courts

A charter may create local courts for local law. In the case before it, a local court applies national guarantees. It does not command national officers, and its opinion does not bind the next case.

A final local judgment on an act bars a second trial of that act in any other court. It does not bar a national court from hearing a victim's claim that the local forum refused, or from obeying this constitution for the parties when a charter's words cannot be obeyed together with it.
