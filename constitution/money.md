# Money and debt

The nation's chief job is to protect life and property. The cost of that protection tracks the land under protection. The nation may tax land. It may also lay a security tariff, or an import or export control, only as the [defense](defense.md) article allows. It taxes nothing else.

Debt is bondage. Capital works are paid when they are authorized, from money already collected. Future citizens are not the collateral.

## Money

The nation does not mint money and does not dictate what money private parties use. No level of government makes an instrument legal tender for private debts, and no level of government issues money.

The treasury accepts any instrument that meets all of the following tests, and it does not favor one qualifying instrument over another:

- the payer can prove a stated parcel's assessment was paid, without revealing other balances or a transaction history
- the treasury can verify the proof without publishing the payer's identity
- the instrument is not issued by the nation or by a sub-jurisdiction
- the payment becomes final under a published finality rule
- the instrument is divisible enough to pay an assessment

A statute publishes the method of applying those tests. Adding an instrument that meets them is ministerial. Private trade may use instruments the treasury does not accept.

A treasury, national or local, may hold several accepted instruments, or may convert what it receives into one. If it keeps only one, it may require payment to itself in that instrument, leaving the exchange to the payer, or it may accept several and do the exchange itself. Either choice is about money paid to that treasury. It does not tell private parties what they must take from each other, and the instrument still has to meet the tests above.

## The land tax

The base is unimproved value and the cost of protecting the parcel. Size matters only insofar as it changes those two. Improvements are not in the base. Building, planting, and repairing are not punished by the national tax.

Frontier parcels may be taxed at the frontier rate, and only under a frontier list enacted as a statute. Each parcel on the list needs a written reason that its defense cost is materially higher. The voters judge that reason when they accept or reject the list. A court does not strike a parcel because it doubts the motive. Until a list is enacted, every parcel pays the ordinary rate.

The rate and formula are an enduring statute. A failed renewal leaves the formula in force, so a missed or a rejected renewal cannot abolish the tax. Setting the rate at zero or repealing the formula takes a grave ballot. The renewal is proposed and debated every sunset period.

The tax is secured only by the parcel. There is no deficiency judgment against the owner. Failure to pay ends in the auction the taxation statute describes.

Only a citizen, or a legal entity whose beneficial owners are all citizens, holds title to land. A transfer that would put title in anyone else does not take effect. The person who would have taken title has one year to cause a lawful transfer, after which the parcel is sold under the taxation statute and the surplus is returned. Until the sale, the land tax is still owed and the non-citizen has no vote. A legal entity pays the tax and has no vote. Its citizen principal answers in court.

Assessment inputs that are not a person's identity are public. The rest follows the privacy article.

## What the nation never taxes

The nation never taxes:

- trade, including retail purchases, barter, gambling, and human labor and services, except a security tariff enacted under the defense article
- personal property
- capital gains
- income

No level of government taxes net worth, a bank balance, or a financial holding. A wealth tax needs a picture of a person's finances. Getting that picture means searching private affairs or compelling the person to report them. Neither is a taxing power. The privacy article and the right to silence bind the nation and every charter on this point.

A sub-jurisdiction may tax income from an act inside its territory, a sale inside its territory, personal property situated inside its territory, and a transaction performed inside its territory, to fund a charter program. It may not tax an act performed outside its territory. The nation may not levy those taxes either, and it may not do so by calling the tax a fee on a monopoly it invented.

## Spending

A spending proposal merges only when receipts on hand, plus the unspent reserve, cover it. The check is arithmetic on published figures. The reserve cannot exceed the reserve cap and is spent only as the executive article allows.

The budget is balanced because unbalanced text cannot be enacted. There is no debt ceiling to raise. There is no borrowing.

National funds are not spent on education, libraries, health care, housing, food, or income. They pay for the duties in the scope article, the per-diem and pay statutes that those duties require, restitution of national takings, and counsel required by the rights article.

## Sub-jurisdictions and the grant problem

The nation does not tax the people in order to offer the money back to a sub-jurisdiction that adopts a preferred policy. That bargain is how a limited government buys powers it was refused.

The nation does not assume local obligations, guarantee them, or borrow on behalf of a charter. Local governments that want a service raise the money from their own people, under the federalism article, before they spend it.
