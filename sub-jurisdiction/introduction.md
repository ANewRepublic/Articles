# Sub-jurisdictions

A sub-jurisdiction is a mini-government formed by people on contiguous land. The nation protects life and property and keeps a few processes. The sub-jurisdiction may do more. It may subsidize, it may serve, and it may tax things the nation may not tax, if the citizens who hold its vote accept the tax by the constitutional ballot. It may not tax wealth, a bank balance, or an act performed outside its territory. It may not punish an outside sender for delivering a virtual product to a resident. It may regulate a physical good that arrives inside the territory.

It may not violate the national guarantees. Exit without a toll, the rights in the national constitution, no debt, no self-written emergency, and no secret law are the core of those guarantees. The binding list is the [federalism](../constitution/federalism.md) article. If this folder and that article ever differ, the article governs.

People who want none of this live on land with no charter. That choice is protected.

Local law uses the national legislative mechanics: a public repository, frozen proposals, delegates, receipt-free votes, sunsets, and ballots counted among the citizens who hold that territory's vote. Each resident citizen holds one. A charter may extend one vote to other citizens on a general condition, such as owning land there. The national text is present as a read-only input. A local commit that tries to amend it is void. A court does not delete a local commit.

The [charter pattern](charter.md) is a starting skeleton, not a charter in force.
