# Open questions

> [!NOTE]
> This file is commentary. It is not law.

The constitution leaves these undecided on purpose. Filling one of them is a constitutional change or a new statute, not an inference from silence.

## Human life before birth

**Person** means a living human being who has been born, for citizenship, voting, and national crimes. Abortion is not a national crime. A sub-jurisdiction may protect human life before birth more strongly, and may restrict a practice inside its own territory. It may not compel a procedure, and it may not stop a person from traveling. Extending **person** would make killing the unborn a national question. That extension is not made here.

## Capital punishment

No statute authorizes a death sentence. The maximum national penalty is incarceration for life. Whether capital punishment should exist is undecided, not quietly adopted.

## Secession

A person may leave. A border parcel may leave a sub-jurisdiction under the federalism article. Whether an entire sub-jurisdiction may leave the nation, and on what ballot, is undecided. Armed resistance is not a lawful substitute for that missing rule.

## Buying the parcel at the assessed value

Appeals and arm's-length resale can pull an assessment down. A stronger rule, used in land-tax theory, would require the taxing authority to buy the parcel at the value it asserts. That rule is not adopted. It would make the nation a standing buyer of land.

## The rate

The land-tax rate is not chosen in this draft. The founding commit or a later statute sets it. Zero is lawful and starves the duties in the scope article. The number is a political decision left explicit so it is not hidden inside a formula.

## Who may enter

Citizens enter by right. Until an entry statute exists, the council may admit or refuse non-citizens and may require them to leave after notice, with a hearing for anyone who claims citizenship. What a future entry statute should say — work, refuge, numbers, removal procedure — is undecided beyond that floor.
