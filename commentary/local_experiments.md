# Local experiments

> [!NOTE]
> This file is commentary. It is not law. The limits are in the [federalism](../constitution/federalism.md) article.

The point of a sub-jurisdiction is that a successful nation will attract people who do not agree. They should be able to try opposite policies without first winning a national office.

## Employment

The nation sets no minimum wage, no tip rule, and no rule for or against unions. Private contracts govern. A charter may set employment rules for future contracts inside its territory. It cannot rewrite contracts already made, and it cannot stop a person from leaving to work under another charter or under none.

## Networks

The nation has no net-neutrality rule. Owners of private networks set their own terms. A sub-jurisdiction may set the terms of conduits it owns, the way it sets the terms of roads it owns. It may not seize a private network or compel a person to carry another person's speech.

## Schools and the poor

A charter may fund schools if a family can take the same funding to a private school or to education at home. A charter may subsidize housing, food, or care for the poor. Those are programs. They are not rights, and the people who do not want them do not have to win a national election to avoid them. They live somewhere else, or they stay on unorganized land and do without the program.

## What experiments do not include

Disarming residents or blocking armed passage, a jail or courtroom gun ban without armed guards at a detection booth, a speech rule inside a home, an exit fee, a debtor's jail, a civic oath to the town, a wealth tax, a tax or penalty on an act done outside the town, a camera network shared into a national movement record, conscription, and a bond issue. A town may be religious. It may require its own officers to belong to its religion, hold public ceremonies, and keep religious symbols on its property. It may not put that test on residents, voters, jurors, or people passing through, and it may not forbid private worship or a congregation meeting on private land. Those limits exist because a local majority plus a locked door, or a set of towns pooling powers the nation is denied, stops being a voluntary experiment. A traveler is not prosecuted for a purely local rule that was not posted at the place, unless the wrong is force, theft, fraud, or trespass. The town's law is the public repository, which anyone can search.
