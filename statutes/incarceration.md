# Incarceration

This statute governs every jail in the nation, national or local. It is enduring. A renewal is debated at each sunset, and a failed renewal leaves these rules in force. Repealing them takes a grave ballot. A sub-jurisdiction may be more lenient. It may not be harsher than the rules in force, and it may not be harsher than the rights article if this statute is ever repealed.

## Order of purposes

Confinement is used in this order of priority:

1. Protection of other people's lives and property, by separating a person who has been convicted of a dangerous act.
2. Restitution to a victim.
3. Deterrence.

Rehabilitation may be offered to a person who wants it. A person is not held past the jury's term in order to finish a program. Punishment as pain, beyond protection, restitution, and deterrence, is not a purpose.

## Conditions

Confinement provides safety, food, sanitation, medical care sufficient to prevent death or serious permanent injury, and a way to petition a court. It does not provide a life of comfort, and it does not inflict cruelty, humiliation, or forced idleness as a punishment.

A confined citizen is given the private voting terminal and a way to communicate with counsel and with a court. Those channels are not monitored except under a warrant the privacy article would allow for a person outside, after a charge.

Debt and inability to pay a fine or restitution are not reasons to confine, and not reasons to extend confinement.

## Labor

A confined person may be offered work and may refuse it.

Work may be required only as restitution to the victim of that person's crime, credited at a published rate against the restitution owed, and ended when the restitution is paid or the term ends, whichever comes first. The worker is not sold to a private master and not used as a free labor force for the state.

## Local jails

A sub-jurisdiction that confines a person follows this statute and the local cap. A national court hears habeas corpus from any local jail. A jail that does not produce the person and the lawful judgment releases the person.
