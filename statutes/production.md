# Production and waste

A person who produces goods that become waste is responsible for the cost of disposing of that waste without damaging other people's persons or property. The producer funds disposal capacity sufficient for the goods it produces.

Paying for disposal does not excuse damage the waste still causes. A person whose body or property is physically damaged may sue in a national court under the [civil rule](../constitution/criminal.md) in the criminal article. A charter cannot permit the damage.

This statute is national because waste moves across charter borders. It is not a plan for production, a quota, or a style code. Aesthetic rules, recycling goals beyond disposal of the producer's own goods, and industrial policy are outside it. A sub-jurisdiction may try those on its own territory, and still cannot legalize physical damage to an owner who has not agreed.

A producer may meet the duty by operating disposal, by paying a disposal provider, or by making goods that do not impose a disposal cost. The duty follows the goods. A contract may allocate the cost between producer and buyer. It does not allocate the damage onto a neighbor who was not a party.
