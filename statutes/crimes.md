# National crimes

This statute is the closed list of national crimes. It is enduring. A renewal is debated at each sunset, and a failed renewal leaves the list in force. Adding an offense, removing an offense, or raising a maximum or a minimum takes a grave ballot under the legislature article. Lowering a maximum or a minimum takes an ordinary ballot.

Every offense below requires a guilty act and a guilty mind. A mistake about a fact is a defense. Ignorance of the enacted text is not.

The jury sets the term inside the range stated for the offense, under the judiciary article. An accepted plea is sentenced at the minimum. Restitution to a victim is ordered alongside the term when the convicted person can pay it, and inability to pay restitution is not a reason to add years.

## Against persons

**Murder.** Intentionally killing a person, or killing a person in the course of a violent felony the offender chose to commit. Range: 20 years to life.

**Reckless killing.** Causing a person's death by a conscious disregard of a substantial risk of death. Range: 5 years to 20 years.

**Intentional serious injury.** Intentionally causing a lasting serious injury to a person's body. Range: 5 years to 15 years.

**Enslavement.** Holding a person in slavery or involuntary servitude, or trafficking a person for that purpose. Range: 20 years to life.

**Rape.** Sexually penetrating another person without that person's consent. Penetration is of the vulva, anus, or mouth by a sexual organ, or of the vulva or anus by an object. Range: 1 year to 5 years.

Consent is a willing agreement at the time of the act, given by words or by conduct. It is proved or disproved by evidence of that time. A later change of mind, and a later denial of an agreement the person gave, are not the absence of consent. A conviction cannot rest on that later statement alone.

A person who is conscious can consent. Alcohol or another intoxicant that the person took, while the person remains conscious, does not by itself make the person unable to consent. Sex between people who are intoxicated and conscious is not rape on the ground that either did not know what they were doing.

A person cannot consent when that person is a minor, is unconscious or asleep, or has been rendered unable to control their body or unable to choose by a drug or other substance that another person administered without that person's knowledge or against that person's will. A conscious refusal at the time is the absence of consent.

When the jury finds one or more of the following in the same episode, it adds one term of 1 year to 5 years. The added term is once for the episode, not once for each fact:

- the other person is a minor
- the other person is unconscious or asleep, unless an incapacitating dose is charged for that same condition
- the offender uses violence, or a present threat of unlawful violence, unless that violence or threat is itself a completed offense on this list charged for the same episode

No other fact raises the rape term. Intoxication that leaves a person conscious, a later regret, and the absence of a lasting injury are not aggravation.

A person convicted of rape who has testes is castrated by surgical removal of the testes. The procedure waits until the time to appeal has passed, or until the appeal is decided and the conviction stands. It is in addition to the term. It is not imposed for an attempt or a conspiracy. A person who does not have testes is not subjected to a substitute surgery. No sub-jurisdiction orders this procedure. The jail provides ordinary surgical care and does not add any other surgery.

**Incapacitating dose.** Knowingly administering a drug or other substance to a person, without that person's knowledge or against that person's will, and thereby rendering that person unconscious, unable to control their body, or unable to choose. Range: 2 years to 10 years.

**Sexual abuse of a minor.** Engaging in sexual conduct with a minor, or causing a minor to engage in sexual conduct. Conduct that is rape is charged as rape and not also as this offense. Range: 5 years to 15 years.

**Sexual abuse material of a minor.** Producing, distributing, or knowingly possessing a visual record of sexual abuse of a minor. A minor who is depicted, and who possesses the record of their own abuse, does not commit this offense by that possession. Range: 5 years to 20 years.

## Against property by force

**Armed taking.** Taking property from a person by violence or by a present threat of violence. Range: 5 years to 20 years.

Damage to property without that violence is not a national crime. It is a civil wrong in national court, and it may be a local crime within the local cap.

## Arms

The disabilities and the transfer rule are defined in the [rights](../constitution/rights.md) article. This list is how they are punished. A local conviction is not a firearms disability.

**Possession under a firearms disability.** Possessing an arm while a firearms disability is in force. Range: 1 year to 5 years.

**Transfer without an eligibility proof.** Transferring an arm without the eligibility proof the rights article requires. A transfer the rights article exempts is not this offense. Range: 1 year to 3 years.

**False disability petition.** In a petition for a mental-condition firearms disability, knowingly stating an act that did not happen, or naming as a ground that the person keeps arms, wants arms, or holds a political or religious opinion. Range: 1 year to 5 years.

## Against the constitution's machinery

**Treason.** As defined in the defense article. Range: 20 years to life.

**Process corruption.** Buying, selling, or coercing a vote or a delegation; paying a delegate or an officer for an official act outside the open delegator channel or the enacted salary; enrollment fraud; a false declaration of residence made to enter a ballot; interfering with a private voting terminal or with a sortition draw; drawing witness wages the witness did not lose. Range: 2 years to 10 years.

**Retaliation against exit.** Detaining a person because the person is leaving a sub-jurisdiction or the nation, or seizing personal property at the border because the person is leaving. Range: 2 years to 10 years.

**Unlawful surveillance.** Directing bulk collection, keeping a prohibited identity or biometric catalog, building or pooling a record of identified persons' movements, including by sharing camera data across jurisdictions, or querying a person's information outside the privacy article. Range: 2 years to 10 years.

**False enactment.** Publishing as law a commit the governance record does not name, or refusing, while under a duty, to publish the commit it does name. Range: 2 years to 10 years.

## Common rules

An attempt to commit an offense on this list, with a substantial step and the intent to complete it, has a maximum of one-half the completed maximum and a minimum of one-half the completed minimum, rounded down to a whole year. Where the completed maximum is life, the attempt maximum is 20 years. Where the rounded minimum is zero, the attempt minimum is zero. Castration is not imposed for an attempt.

A conspiracy of two or more persons to commit an offense on this list, plus an overt act, has the same range as an attempt. Castration is not imposed for a conspiracy.

When a person commits rape and, in the same episode, commits another completed offense on this list, the jury sets each term inside its own range and the terms are added. The rape term does not absorb the other term. On a plea to a charge that includes the added rape term, the minimum is the sum of the minima of the terms the charge includes.

No offense on this list reaches conduct before this statute's enactment. None of these offenses is a strict-liability regulatory crime.

Abortion is not an offense on this list. Whether a future constitutional change extends **person** is an [open question](../commentary/open_questions.md), not a crime created by silence.
