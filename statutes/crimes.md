# National crimes

This statute is the closed list of national crimes. It is enduring. A renewal is debated at each sunset, and a failed renewal leaves the list in force. Adding an offense, removing an offense, or raising a maximum takes a grave ballot under the legislature article. Lowering a maximum takes an ordinary ballot.

Every offense below requires a guilty act and a guilty mind. A mistake about a fact is a defense. Ignorance of the enacted text is not.

The jury sets the term at or under the maximum, under the judiciary article. Restitution to a victim is ordered alongside the term when the convicted person can pay it, and inability to pay restitution is not a reason to add years.

## Against persons

**Murder.** Intentionally killing a person, or killing a person in the course of a violent felony the offender chose to commit. Maximum: life.

**Reckless killing.** Causing a person's death by a conscious disregard of a substantial risk of death. Maximum: 20 years.

**Intentional serious injury.** Intentionally causing a lasting serious injury to a person's body. Maximum: 15 years.

**Enslavement.** Holding a person in slavery or involuntary servitude, or trafficking a person for that purpose. Maximum: life.

**Sexual abuse of a minor.** Engaging in sexual conduct with a minor, or causing a minor to engage in sexual conduct. Maximum: life.

**Sexual abuse material of a minor.** Producing, distributing, or knowingly possessing a visual record of sexual abuse of a minor. A minor who is depicted, and who possesses the record of their own abuse, does not commit this offense by that possession. Maximum: 20 years.

## Against property by force

**Armed taking.** Taking property from a person by violence or by a present threat of violence. Maximum: 20 years.

Damage to property without that violence is not a national crime. It is a civil wrong in national court, and it may be a local crime within the local cap.

## Against the constitution's machinery

**Treason.** As defined in the defense article. Maximum: life.

**Process corruption.** Buying, selling, or coercing a vote or a delegation; paying a delegate or an officer for an official act outside the open delegator channel or the enacted salary; enrollment fraud; a false declaration of residence made to enter a ballot; interfering with a private voting terminal or with a sortition draw. Maximum: 10 years.

**Retaliation against exit.** Detaining a person because the person is leaving a sub-jurisdiction or the nation, or seizing personal property at the border because the person is leaving. Maximum: 10 years.

**Unlawful surveillance.** Directing bulk collection, keeping a prohibited identity or biometric catalog, building or pooling a record of identified persons' movements, including by sharing camera data across jurisdictions, or querying a person's information outside the privacy article. Maximum: 10 years.

**False enactment.** Publishing as law a commit the governance record does not name, or refusing, while under a duty, to publish the commit it does name. Maximum: 10 years.

## Common rules

An attempt to commit an offense on this list, with a substantial step and the intent to complete it, has a maximum of one-half the completed maximum. Where the completed maximum is life, the attempt maximum is 20 years.

A conspiracy of two or more persons to commit an offense on this list, plus an overt act, has the same maximum as an attempt.

No offense on this list reaches conduct before this statute's enactment. None of these offenses is a strict-liability regulatory crime.

Abortion is not an offense on this list. Whether a future constitutional change extends **person** is an [open question](../commentary/open_questions.md), not a crime created by silence.
